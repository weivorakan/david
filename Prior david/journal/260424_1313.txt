Based on the Al Brooks Price Action methodology, a **High 2** is a specific type of pullback setup used primarily in bull trends or at the bottom of a trading range. Here is the detailed breakdown:

### 1. The Bar-by-Bar Mechanics
A High 2 is defined by a specific sequence of price action during a correction:
*   **High 1:** First, there is a **High 1**, which is a bar with a high above the high of the prior bar (occurring during a bull flag or near the bottom of a trading range).
*   **The Intermediate Bar:** Following the High 1, there is a bar (or several) that forms a **lower high**.
*   **High 2:** The next bar whose high is above the high of that prior "lower high" bar completes the **High 2** pattern.

### 2. Structural Meaning
A High 2 represents the **end of the second leg** of a correction. While a High 1 represents the first attempt by bulls to resume the trend, the High 2 represents the second attempt after the first attempt (the High 1) failed to sustain the move.

### 3. Requirements for a High-Quality Setup
A High 2 is not a guaranteed buy signal on its own. For it to be a reliable "with-trend" setup, the following context is necessary:
*   **Prior Strength:** There must be evidence of earlier strength, such as a strong bull trend bar or a High 1 leg that breaks a trend line.
*   **Trend Line Break:** Ideally, there should be at least a tiny trend line break between the High 1 and the High 2 to indicate that trend traders are still active in the market.
*   **Avoid "Bad" Setups:** A High 2 is a poor setup if the preceding bars are almost entirely bear bars (e.g., five out of six prior bars being bear bars), as this suggests the momentum has shifted too heavily toward the bears.

### 4. Contextual Application
*   **In a Bull Trend:** It is one of the most reliable with-trend setups, signaling the end of a pullback.
*   **In a Trading Range:** It can be a buy signal, but specifically when it occurs at the **bottom of the range**, rather than near the top.

### 5. Failure and Evolution
If a High 2 forms but the market fails to reverse upward—specifically, if a bar trades below the low of the High 2 signal bar—the pattern has failed. In this case, the downward correction is likely to continue, and traders will look for the pattern to evolve into a **High 3** (a wedge variant) or a **High 4** pullback.
